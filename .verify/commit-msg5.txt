Raise the frame rate by cutting shaded pixels, not meshes

Profiled first. The room is 198 meshes and 6,638 triangles, and the whole
animate() body measured 1.44 ms of a 16.67 ms budget - 8.6%. So geometry was
never the bottleneck and LOD or instancing would have bought nothing. The cost
is fill rate, which scales with the square of the pixel ratio.

- PerformanceManager measures real frame time and scales the drawing buffer
  between 0.5x and 2x to hold 45 fps. Halving the ratio removes three quarters of
  the shaded pixels. It drops fast (x0.82) and climbs slowly (x1.08), needs two
  agreeing samples to move, and ignores frames over 250 ms so one stall while a
  model parses cannot collapse it to the floor. Lands exactly on the limits, or
  a multiplicative climb would stall just short of native forever.
  The FPS and current scale show in #perf-readout, amber below 40.
- The shadow map was being redrawn every frame although the room is static.
  autoUpdate is off and it refreshes every 4th frame, with markWorldChanged()
  forcing an immediate redraw on pickup or drop so shadows stay correct.
  2048 -> 1024, which is ~8 mm per texel across the 8 m shadow volume.
- PCFSoftShadowMap -> PCFShadowMap. The soft filter takes roughly four times
  the shadow samples per lit pixel, in a scene that is entirely lit pixels.
- The POST sequence repainted a 2D canvas and re-uploaded the whole monitor
  texture every frame. Now five times a second, still smooth over a few seconds.
- The hover raycast was the single most expensive thing in the frame and was
  redone 60 times a second, but its result only changes when the camera or the
  world does. It is now cached against the camera pose and skipped when nothing
  moved: 1.44 ms -> 0.006 ms standing still, 0.073 ms while sweeping the bench.
- Also drops three Vector3 allocations per frame, gathers the raycast target
  list once instead of spreading three arrays per frame, and caches the HUD
  nodes so textContent and style are only written when the value changes.

Two real bugs surfaced while doing this:

- A picked-up part could still be hovered and clicked again.
  ShelfHardware.hideItem() only sets visible = false, and three.js r174
  raycasts invisible objects - verified, two hits either way. The hit loop now
  skips anything invisible through its own ancestors.
- The crosshair ray lagged a frame behind, because setFromCamera needs the
  camera matrixWorld and that is only refreshed by renderer.render(), which
  runs after the raycast. update() now refreshes it first.

Frame timings vary widely between runs - a clean synthetic raycast measured
0.03 to 0.20 ms - so the numbers above are medians, not single samples.
